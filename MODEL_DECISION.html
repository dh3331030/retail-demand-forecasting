Project guide

Why we chose ARIMA

ARIMA is the simpler final forecasting choice, supported by strong retrospective performance. It uses the history of retail growth itself and avoids the extra data, timing and maintenance requirements of the discarded news integration.

“Best” needs a boundary. The surviving evidence does not support “ARIMA had the lowest error among everything tested” or “ARIMA was selected on an untouched test set.” Some complex combinations scored slightly better after extensive experimentation.

What ARIMA means in plain language

ARIMA predicts the next value from patterns in previous values and previous forecast errors, allowing differencing when needed. The seasonal option also considers patterns repeating every 12 months. We forecast monthly growth, calculated as 100 times the logarithm of the ratio between consecutive retail-volume indices.

This is not one fixed ARIMA order for all countries. forecast::auto.arima chooses an order separately for each country and forecast origin. The retained core specification uses all continuous available growth history, monthly frequency 12, seasonal=TRUE, stepwise=TRUE and approximation=TRUE. The function is allowed to select a nonseasonal model if appropriate. The comparison also includes a 2016-onward variant; its performance does not silently change the chosen core.

How the decision developed

  1. Original chronological comparison. In the earlier 2023 validation exercise, Zero growth had MAE 1.02566; Elastic net five had MAE 1.02688 and lower RMSE. Under the MAE-first rule, Zero growth won. This was an earlier candidate set and procedure, not proof that ARIMA lost a comparable contest. Original validation evidence.
  2. Overnight expansion, 26–27 October. The agent tested retail-history statistical models, shorter-history ARIMA settings and 1,872 approved-source regression settings, followed by combinations and adaptive choices. The user had allowed 2023 in training and requested the lowest 2024-2025 errors. Thus the later ranking became retrospective. The overnight chat explicitly reported core combinations with MAE 0.77876 / RMSE 1.10141 and another with MAE 0.78640 / RMSE 1.09625; it did not establish these as pure-ARIMA scores or a proven optimum. Some searches were still running at the last substantive update.
  3. Saved 1 November comparison. The surviving forecast-level evidence gives full-history ARIMA MAE 0.786403 / RMSE 1.108183 across 120 targets. Zero growth scores 0.919684 / 1.267812. A fixed blend scores 0.783816 / 1.097866; two core gates also improve some aggregate metrics. These alternatives demonstrate why “absolute lowest error” is the wrong justification for choosing standalone ARIMA. Saved comparison and underlying forecasts.
  4. Final scope, 3 November. Laya and unnecessary experimentation were removed from the final scope. ARIMA is retained as a transparent retail-only baseline with a strong error reduction over simple alternatives. Supporting economic/conflict analysis remains separate. Simplicity is a design choice, not a statistically proven optimal trade-off.
  5. Fresh reproduction. The final workflow rebuilds the raw data, refits both ARIMA variants and standard benchmarks, compares the full-history predictions with all 120 saved forecasts, and publishes the current scores. Reproduction check and new comparison.

What the final rerun confirmed

The fresh run ranks full-history ARIMA first on both MAE and RMSE among the ten retained standard methods, on the same 120 targets. Its MAE is 0.786403 and RMSE is 1.108183. All 120 forecasts match the previous saved ARIMA predictions to numerical precision (maximum difference below 0.00000000000001). Its MAE is about 14.5% below Zero growth. These findings support the project’s emphasis: accuracy with a simpler, reproducible model.

The defensible statement for the report

We retain a country-specific automatic ARIMA approach as the final core model because it offers strong retrospective forecasting accuracy using only retail history, with substantially less complexity than the experimental combinations and news extension. We evaluate all methods on the same 2024–2025 country-month targets. Since this period informed earlier model development, these comparisons do not constitute an untouched test of future performance.

What the migration can and cannot recover

The complete outputs from earlier experimental searches are not included in this release. The retained evidence supports a transparent account of model selection, but does not claim to reproduce every prior experiment.

Evaluation details to keep clear

  • Five countries × 24 months = 120 final retrospective targets. The additional 2023 scores in the new run are diagnostics of the current fixed candidates; they do not rewrite the original selection history.
  • Training expands monthly. Actual earlier months within 2024–2025 become available to subsequent one-month fits; there is no look-ahead to the target month in the fitting code.
  • The source extract is revised and extends beyond 2025. Post-2025 rows are excluded from all these forecast fits. Earlier revised rows still do not reconstruct what was published at each historical origin.
  • MAE is the average absolute miss; RMSE gives greater weight to large misses. The reported units are log percentage points of monthly retail growth.
  • A future, genuinely uninspected evaluation would be needed for a stronger generalisation claim.
