Questions the presenters should be able to answer

Why did a simple method win?

Monthly retail growth has little predictable signal under these delayed inputs and tested specifications. The evidence shows no consistent benefit from extra complexity; it does not show that all complex forecasting is useless.

Which is the selected model, and which is the test winner?

Zero growth is the validation-selected primary model and remains the validation RMSE winner in the rerun. Country mean has the lowest pooled test error among six fixed comparators in both result sets. Selecting the test winner after looking at test scores would require another unseen evaluation period.

Did conflict improve accuracy?

Not consistently. Death-weighted conflict improved one linear validation comparison, but the matched forest addition worsened validation and pooled test RMSE. The test errors are 1.2712 without conflict and 1.2825 with it.

Was this a forecast that could really have been issued in January 2024?

No exact historical vintage is reconstructed. Fixed lag conventions are applied to revised downloads, and complete 2023 validation was not yet available at the earliest 2024 origin. This is a retrospective evaluation with publication-delay assumptions.

Why are earlier test outcomes used for later predictions?

Each model is refitted monthly using only outcomes eligible under the three-month retail reporting rule. Past eligible outcomes can enter later training; future outcomes cannot. Model specifications remain frozen.

What does a prediction of 0.12 mean?

It is 0.12 log percentage points, approximately 0.12% monthly growth, not 12%. The model predicts growth rather than the retail index level.

What business decision follows?

Use simple benchmarks as a serious reference and avoid adding data/model complexity without demonstrated gains. The project does not validate an operational inventory or revenue policy, and its index is sector volume rather than a particular retailer’s demand.
